# Scorecard

Four dimensions, 0 to 5 each. These are not invented categories. They are the Narrative Engagement Measure of Busselle & Bilandzic (2009), an empirically derived and validated instrument with reported reliability at or above α = .80 for the full scale. `[M]`

Score the piece, not the sentence. For long work, score the opening separately from the whole, because exit risk is not evenly distributed.

## The four dimensions

### 1. Narrative understanding

Can the reader follow what is happening, who is involved, and why it matters, without re-reading?

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Reader cannot say what the piece is about after the first paragraph |
| 2 | Followable but requires effort or backtracking |
| 4 | Clear on first pass; the stakes are legible |
| 5 | Clear on first pass and the reader can predict what question comes next |

### 2. Attentional focus

Does the prose hold attention, or does the mind wander?

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Attention drops inside the first sentence |
| 2 | Holds through the opening, drifts in the body |
| 4 | Holds throughout; no dead paragraph |
| 5 | Holds throughout and the reader resists interruption |

The diagnostic is the slot mechanic. Walk each paragraph and ask what it closed and what it opened. Any paragraph that did neither is where attention drops.

### 3. Narrative presence

Does the reader feel inside the situation rather than outside reading about it? This is transportation, the mechanism Green & Brock (2000) tied to belief change.

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Pure abstraction; nothing to stand in |
| 2 | Occasional concrete anchor, mostly summary |
| 4 | A place, a person, or a mechanism the reader can hold |
| 5 | Reader loses track of the prose as prose |

Marketing counts. Transportation effects did not differ reliably by fictionality in the meta-analytic evidence, so a product page can score here and should.

### 4. Emotional engagement

Does the reader care what happens? Not "is it emotive." Does the outcome matter to them.

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Nothing at stake for anyone |
| 2 | Stakes stated but not felt |
| 4 | Stakes belong to someone specific and the reader wants an outcome |
| 5 | Reader has taken a side |

## Thresholds

| Context | Minimum per dimension | Minimum total |
|---|---|---|
| Chat reply, internal note | 2 | 10 of 20 |
| README, docs, release notes | 3 | 13 of 20 |
| Blog post, article, newsletter | 3 | 15 of 20 |
| Marketing, landing page, ad | 4 | 16 of 20 |
| Fiction, essay, chapter | 4 | 17 of 20 |
| Any opening, any form | 4 | 16 of 20 |

Below threshold means revise and re-score. Do not ship a draft that failed its own gate.

## Recording a score

Four numbers, one line, plus the weakest dimension and the fix.

```
Peitho: understanding 4, focus 3, presence 2, emotion 3 = 12/20 (blog needs 15)
Weakest: presence. Paragraphs 2 to 5 are all summary. Add a named instance in para 2.
```

## What the score is not

It is not a prediction of success. Narrative persuasion effects are real but modest, around r = .17 to .23 (Braddock & Dillard 2016), and the largest structural study found no link between normative structure and popularity. A 17 of 20 means the piece does the things that produce immersion. It does not mean the piece will do well.

Say that plainly if anyone asks what the number means.
